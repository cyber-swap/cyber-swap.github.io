What CyberSwap Is

CyberSwap is a self-custody swap interface that lets you exchange one token for another straight from your wallet, pricing every trade against liquidity pool reserves rather than a book of buy and sell orders. There is no sign-up and no deposit into a custodial account — you connect a wallet you already control, choose the token you hold and the token you want, and the interface quotes a rate pulled live from on-chain pools. When you confirm, a single transaction swaps the tokens atomically: either the full trade settles or it reverts, so you never end up holding half a trade.

How an Automated Market Maker Prices a Swap

Most swap interfaces, CyberSwap included, route trades through automated market maker pools rather than matching individual buyers with sellers. Each pool holds a reserve of two tokens, and a pricing formula sets the exchange rate from the ratio between them — the more of one token the pool already holds relative to the other, the cheaper that token is to buy. The mechanics behind this model are documented in detail in the Uniswap protocol documentation, which many AMM designs trace their logic back to.

Liquidity Pools Are the Counterparty

When you swap on CyberSwap you are trading against a liquidity pool, not against another person waiting on the other side. Anyone can deposit a pair of tokens into a pool and earn a share of the trading fees collected from every swap that passes through it, which is why deeper pools tend to offer tighter pricing. The general concept of trading against pooled liquidity rather than a matched order book is summarized on Wikipedia's decentralized exchange entry.

Price Impact and Slippage Tolerance on CyberSwap

Two numbers decide how much you actually receive from a CyberSwap trade: price impact and slippage tolerance. Price impact is how far the pool's price moves because your own trade changes the ratio of reserves — the larger your trade relative to the pool, the more the price moves against you before the swap finishes. Slippage tolerance is a limit you set yourself, telling the contract the worst exchange rate you will accept between the moment you sign and the moment the transaction is mined.

Why Larger Trades Move the Price More

A small trade against a deep pool barely shifts the reserve ratio, so the quoted rate and the final rate stay close together. A large trade against a shallow pool can shift that ratio substantially, meaning the average price you pay across the trade is worse than the price shown at the very start of the quote. Splitting a large order into smaller trades, or routing through a deeper pool, is the usual way to keep price impact down.

Setting a Slippage Tolerance That Protects You

A slippage tolerance that is too tight can make a trade fail during normal price movement, wasting the gas spent on the attempt. A tolerance that is too loose exposes you to sandwich attacks, where someone trades ahead of and behind your transaction to capture the difference. A moderate default with the option to widen it for volatile pairs is the balance most swap interfaces, CyberSwap included, aim for.

Token Approvals Before a CyberSwap Trade

Swapping an ERC-20 token for the first time on CyberSwap takes two signatures rather than one. The first grants the swap contract an allowance to move a specific token out of your wallet; the second actually executes the swap itself. Native assets like ETH never need this step because they are not governed by the ERC-20 allowance standard.

Approve and Swap Are Separate Signatures

An approval signature only sets a permission — it does not move any funds on its own. The swap signature that follows is the transaction that actually exchanges your tokens. Keeping these as two distinct steps means you can review exactly what you are permitting before any token leaves your wallet.

Limit the Allowance You Grant

Many interfaces default to an unlimited allowance for convenience, letting you skip the approval step on future trades of the same token. A limited allowance, sized to the trade in front of you, is safer: if the contract you approved is ever compromised, the most it can move is the amount you actually authorized, not your entire balance.

Routing a CyberSwap Trade to the Best Pool

The same token pair can exist across several pools with different fee tiers and different depths, so CyberSwap compares the available routes and picks the one that returns the most tokens after fees and price impact — sometimes splitting a trade across more than one pool when that produces a better combined result. A deeper pool at a slightly higher fee tier can still beat a shallow pool at a lower fee tier once price impact is accounted for.

Gas, Native Tokens, and Self-Custody on CyberSwap

Every CyberSwap transaction — approval and swap alike — is settled on-chain, which means it costs gas paid in the network's native token rather than in the tokens being swapped. On an Ethereum-based network that is ETH, and the general mechanics of how gas pricing works are laid out on ethereum.org. If you bridge over to an EVM network built for lower fees, such as Arbitrum, the swap itself works the same way but gas typically costs a small fraction of what it does on the base layer. Throughout all of this your tokens stay in your own wallet; CyberSwap never takes custody, and the only thing you ever sign is a transaction you can read before approving it.

How to Swap on CyberSwap, Step by Step

The flow stays the same for every pair, which is what makes it predictable once you have done it a few times.
  • Connect your self-custody wallet to CyberSwap and select the token you hold and the token you want.
  • Enter the amount; review the quoted rate, the estimated price impact, and the minimum amount you will receive.
  • Set a slippage tolerance appropriate for the pair — tighter for stable pairs, wider for volatile ones.
  • Approve the token if this is your first swap of it, then sign the swap transaction and pay gas in the native token.
  • Confirm the trade settled by checking the resulting balance in your wallet.

Is CyberSwap Safe to Use?

CyberSwap's self-custody design means the asset never passes through a third party, so the risks that remain are the ones you control at the moment you sign. Confirm the token's contract address before trading an unfamiliar pair, read the amount you will receive rather than trusting the headline rate alone, and keep approvals limited to what each trade actually needs.

Checking a Contract Before You Trust It

Before swapping an unfamiliar token, verify its contract address against a trusted source such as Arbiscan rather than trusting a name or logo alone, since anyone can deploy a token that copies both. Matching the address, not the symbol, is what actually confirms you are trading the asset you think you are.

The Rule That Never Changes

No swap interface, wallet or support channel ever needs your seed phrase. CyberSwap only ever asks your wallet to sign a transaction you can read — it never asks you to type or paste your recovery words anywhere, and anyone who does ask is attempting fraud.
What is CyberSwap?
CyberSwap is a self-custody swap interface that exchanges one token for another by pricing trades against automated market maker liquidity pools instead of a matched order book, while your tokens stay in your own wallet.
Does CyberSwap take custody of my tokens?
No. CyberSwap only ever asks your wallet to sign a transaction. You hold your own keys before, during and after every swap.
Why does my CyberSwap trade need two signatures?
The first signature approves a limited allowance for the ERC-20 token being swapped; the second actually executes the swap. Native assets like ETH skip the approval step entirely.
What is price impact on a CyberSwap trade?
Price impact is how far a pool's price moves because your own trade changes the ratio of its reserves. Larger trades against shallower pools move the price more than smaller trades against deeper pools.
How should I set slippage tolerance?
Use a tighter tolerance for stable pairs and a wider one for volatile pairs. Too tight and the trade can fail; too loose and you risk a worse fill than necessary.
What gas token does CyberSwap use?
Gas is paid in the native token of whichever network you are trading on — ETH on Ethereum, or the equivalent native token on other EVM networks such as Arbitrum — never in the tokens being swapped.
How do I know I am swapping the real token?
Verify the contract address on a trusted explorer before trading an unfamiliar pair. Matching the address rather than the name or logo is what actually confirms the token's identity.
